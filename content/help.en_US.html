How to use the map

Click the boxes to read more.

Search

Search results start to appear once you have typed at least 2 characters. Select a search result to zoom to that location.

 Search for addresses, properties, places or detailed development plans...
 Show property. When the function is on, you can click in the map to get the name of the property. The property is also drawn in the map, including all of its parcels. You can also see the owners by clicking the rectangle marked "ÄG" that appears just above where you clicked.
Show information about objects in the map

Click an object in the map to open a box with information about the object.

Zoom and navigate
 Zooms in.
 Zooms out.
 Zooms the map to its initial view.
 Shows your current position in the map (if your device supports it). Clicking twice turns on "follow me", which is shown as active by the arrow turning blue and pointing upwards. If you move the map manually while "follow me" is active, the function is turned off.
 Rotates the map so that north points up. To rotate the map on a computer without a touch screen, hold Shift + Alt, click and drag.
 Use the scale picker in the footer to choose from predefined zoom scales.

Interact with the map

  • You can also zoom with the mouse wheel or by double-clicking in the map.
  • Hold Shift and double-click in the map to zoom out.
  • Pan the map by clicking and dragging in the map.
  • You can also hold Shift and drag a rectangle in the map with the mouse to zoom in to that area (Box Zoom).

Measure
 Turn on the measuring tools, click again to turn them off.
 Measure distances in the map by drawing a line, finish the measurement by double-clicking in the map.
 Measure areas in the map by drawing a polygon, finish the measurement by double-clicking in the map.
 Get the elevation by clicking in the map. From Lantmäteriet's service Höjd Direkt.
 Turn off the segment labels on drawn length and area measurements.
 Draw a buffer around a point. Click anywhere in the map and enter the buffer radius in metres, and a buffer is drawn around the point.
 Remove measurements from the map. Otherwise the measurements stay in the map when the measuring tools are turned off.
 Get an elevation profile for measured distances and areas.

 Open the drawing menu. It is shown at the bottom of the map

Interact with the drawing tool

  • Hold Shift and drag in the map to draw a line or area freehand.
  • Hold Alt and click a vertex to remove it.
Print
 Open the print preview.
 Print settings.

You can add a title above the map. The alignment and size of the title can also be changed.

You can add a description below the map. The alignment and size of the description can also be changed.

The size can be set to A4, A3 or a custom size.

Change the orientation, landscape or portrait.

The resolution can be set to low, medium or high, which corresponds to 75, 150 and 300 dpi. A higher resolution gives a larger file.

Choose a print scale to get a printout to scale.

Buttons for turning appearance/information on and off: margins, created time, scale, north arrow and legend.

Slider for rotating the map.

 Change the map mode. By default the map in the print preview is locked, click the button to be able to pan and zoom the map.
 Slider for rotating the map.
 Minimize the print settings.
 Close the print preview.

The "Save image" button creates an image file that can be pasted into other programs/documents.

The "Create pdf" button creates a PDF that can then be printed or sent to others.

Filter

Tool for filtering visible layers by attribute.

 Open the filter tool.

How to:

  • Choose one of the visible layers in the drop-down list.
  • Decide whether the filter should match "any" (OR) or "all" (AND) of the following by choosing in the drop-down list.
  • Choose what to filter on in the "Attribute" list.
  • Choose how the attribute should be filtered in the "Operator" list.
  • Finally, type in a value.
  • To filter in several ways, click "Add attribute" to add another row of choices.
  • Click the "Filter" button to apply the filter.
  • Click the "Clear" button to remove the filter on the layer selected under "Visible layers".
  • Click the "Clear all" button to remove the filters on all layers.
Under the "Advanced" tab you can write the filter yourself if you know the CQL format.
Under the "My filters" tab you will find all active filters and can remove or open them.
When filtering with "like", use % as a wildcard.

Note! The function is case-sensitive, so the value must be spelled exactly as it is written to get a match.

Compare map views

Tool for comparing different map views. You can compare side by side or through a spyglass. The layers to compare with can be changed in the layer picker window, which can be moved and closed. The regular legend is used to change which backgrounds and layers are shown on the right side (or above the spyglass). How much of the map each side takes up can be adjusted by dragging the blue arrow button at the bottom of the map.

 Open the compare map views tool.
 Choose side-by-side comparison.
 Choose the spyglass.
 Open/close the layer picker window.
Select in the map

Tool for selecting objects in the map layers and exporting the selection to a file. Note! Selecting does not work in all layers. To see whether a layer supports it, click in the layer menu and read the text at the bottom.

 Open the "Select in the map" tool.
 Select objects in the map by clicking them one by one.
 Select objects in the map by clicking and dragging a rectangle over the area you want to include.
 Select objects in the map by clicking and dragging a circle over the area you want to include.
 Select objects in the map by clicking several times to draw an area over the part you want to include. Finish by double-clicking or right-clicking.
 Select objects in the map by clicking an object and then entering the size of the buffer around its geometry to select objects within. Tip! First draw a geometry with the drawing tool where you want to select objects with a buffer. Close the drawing tool, open the "Select in the map" tool and click the drawn geometry with the buffer button.
 Select objects in the map by clicking and drawing a line along what you want to select.
 Open the export window. Choose the format you want. Excel gives all attributes of the selected objects in an ordinary spreadsheet. GeoJSON creates a file with all attributes and geometries that can be used in other GIS programs such as QGIS or dragged into the web map. CSV creates a comma-separated file with all attributes that can be imported into spreadsheet programs or databases. You can also choose to include the geometries as an attribute in a text format. Complex geometries can get large, so untick the checkbox if you do not need them.
 Remove objects from the list.
